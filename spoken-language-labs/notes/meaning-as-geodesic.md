# Meaning as the geodesic

**Status:** discussion record, 2026-11-10. Not book text, not in the paper.

## Definition (author)

The listener does not keep the sound. Each wave moves its knowledge one step through the medium, so listening to an utterance leaves a path, and that path is what the listener records. In Part III the geodesic is the result of learning; here it is what remains of what was heard.

**The meaning of an utterance is its geodesic.**

## What follows

1. **Independent of the language.** A geodesic does not depend on coordinates. If languages are coordinate projections of one manifold, the same meaning in Hebrew and in English is one geodesic seen in two coordinate systems. A translation preserves meaning when it carries one geodesic onto the other.
2. **Independent of tempo.** A geodesic is a curve, whatever the speed along it. The same word spoken slower or faster gives the same waves stretched along the wave index, so it traces the same geodesic at another speed.
3. **Synonyms.** Different waves that lead to the same geodesic.
4. **Ambiguity.** Between two endpoints there can be several geodesics, especially beyond a conjugate point (Part III, Appendix D). One utterance then has several meanings, and the curvature of the medium decides where this happens.
5. **Understanding grows.** The metric is built from the listener's entropy and neuron density, so it changes as the listener learns: the same utterance gives a different geodesic, and is understood differently.
6. **Meaning belongs to a listener.** Two listeners with different media have different metrics, so the same sound gives each a different geodesic. Shared meaning needs their geometries to agree.

## Open points

1. **Endpoints.** Where the path starts and ends: the word, the verse, or the loop (as in the ECG work).
2. **Space or time.** In Part III a geodesic lies in space within one pass, and learning runs over the passes. For a listener the stream is time, one wave per step. Is the record the path in space at the end of the utterance, or the sequence of states over the waves?
3. **Speakers.** A second speaker of the same language changes the waves. Does the geodesic stay the same, as the same word in other coordinates, or does it change?
4. **Sameness.** Two real geodesics are never exactly equal. A measure of closeness between geodesics is needed (length, curvature along the path, entropy reduced along it). It is the measure the refutation test applies: if the geodesics of the same verse are no closer across languages than those of different verses, the hypothesis fails.
